One thing to know

Whoever opens the room becomes the moderator

On the public Jitsi server, the first person into the room is made the moderator — and the moderator is asked to sign in once, with a Google account. Everyone else just types a name and joins; no accounts, no sign-in.

So it is worth deciding who opens it. Daniele opening the room keeps the sign-in on a technical account and leaves Linda free to arrive as a guest — and it puts the recording controls with whoever is managing the session.

The moderator is also the one who can record. Jitsi will not let anyone else start a recording, so whoever opens the room needs to be ready to press ⋮ → Start recording → Dropbox in the first couple of minutes.

For Linda

Holding Air

Air follows Water, and the two are companions. Water is flow, change, what moves. Air is the mind that holds the change — breath, movement, mental clarity.

Water taught that you do not fight the current. Air teaches that you also need somewhere to stand while the current moves. That is what protection is for: not a wall against the world, but a boundary you can feel.

This is the module where protection belongs — grounding, shielding, clearing. It is deliberately second, and deliberately before transformation, because people need to feel safe before they can be asked to do anything difficult. Air is the element of the mind, so it is the right place to talk about what the mind does when it is not protected: it overworks, it plans, it rehearses.

The practice should be simple enough to do at a kitchen table and specific enough to be worth doing. Grounding first — feel the ground, feel the breath. Then shielding: a boundary drawn with the breath, felt in the body, and named. Then clearing: what goes when that boundary is asked to hold less.

The meditation follows the breath as it actually moves rather than instructing anyone to do anything with it. Air is the element of movement without force — which is also what a prayer is.

These are working notes, not your words. Treat them as scaffolding for the teaching you will actually give. Nothing here has been written in your voice.

Recording

Three ways to capture it

Jitsi's own — records everyone, on the server. In the meeting: ⋮ → Start recording → Dropbox. Only a moderator can do this.

Local audio — your Mac's sound, outside the browser. The ● Local Audio button on the console, or ⌘⌥⌃M. The cleanest single track, and it runs for the whole session.

Transcribe — MacWhisper, on this Mac, once the session is done. The console has a Transcribe latest button.
