The Breath of Protection

A working script for the Air session — the teaching, the practice, the meditation and the closing, in the order they run.

A draft in your structure, not your words. The order follows the shape of the Water session — blessing, bowl, breathwork, teaching, working, meditation, circle, closing. Every line here is scaffolding: rewrite it in your own voice, or replace it outright. Print it, or open it on your phone and read from it.
00:00 Arrival 5 min

People arrive as they arrive. Some will be early, some will come in halfway through. Leave the room open and let the settling happen without commentary.

  • Headphones on, if you have them — it keeps the room clear for everyone else.
  • A glass of water, and a dark bowl for scrying.
  • Cameras off is fine, and probably better for the opening.
  • Someone opens the room and becomes the moderator. They start the recording.

The local audio recording on the moderator's machine is worth starting now rather than later. It runs the whole session and gives a clean track of the room.

00:05 Opening blessing & crystal bowl 7 min

The blessing

In the Water session this was addressed to Archangel Michael, the spiritual guides, the divine energy, and the grandmothers of the four directions, for protection and grounding. The Air session can keep that shape and let the element of the day change its texture.

Air is the element we live in between breaths. It moves before thought does. It is the first thing we touch and the last thing we let go of.

A draft opening. The Water blessing gave protection and grounding as its outcome. For Air, the equivalent might be clarity — arriving at the mind rather than dismissing it.

The bowl

The crystal bowl. Then the breath — into the belly, and out, letting go of whatever was carried in. Six cycles, unhurried.

The Water session moved the breath to the belly. For Air it might sit higher in the chest — the natural place of the breath is also the place of the element.

00:12 Grounding breath 6 min

Six slow cycles. Feet on the floor. Breath into the belly. Long enough out that the shoulders drop.

Words to speak

Feel your feet on the floor. All of the weight of you, given to the ground that is holding it up. You do not have to hold yourself up. The ground is doing that.
Now the breath. Not a breath you are making — a breath that is already happening, that you are simply noticing. In through the nose. Out through the mouth. Let the out-breath be longer than the in-breath.

This is the handover from the opening into the teaching. It is not a technique to learn yet — it is the body arriving before the mind starts working.

00:18 Teaching · Air 13 min

Air is the element of the mind. It governs breath, movement, thought, speech — everything that travels. It is also the element of the body that is most easily ignored: you can go days without noticing a single breath, and the mind will go on working the whole time.

Three threads, in this order

  • Air moves before thought does. The breath changes before you decide anything. Where the breath goes, the mind follows — which is why a practice that works on the breath works on the mind.
  • Water was flow; Air is what holds it. The Water session said not to fight the current. Air is where you need somewhere to stand while the current moves. That is what protection is for — not a wall against the world, but a boundary you can feel.
  • What an unprotected mind does. It overworks. It plans. It rehearses conversations that have not happened. Air is the element that notices this happening, which is why protection comes before power in this course.

A story to hold

Worth having one piece of myth or image in reserve for Air — the way Sedna carried the Water session. Air has no single obvious figure, which is itself interesting: the element that moves is the hardest one to pin down.

This is the part Linda knows and I don't. Put her own story here.

00:31 Practice · Grounding & shielding 11 min

A practice simple enough to do at a kitchen table and specific enough to be worth doing. Three stages, and people can do it in any order.

One · Ground

Both feet flat. Feel the weight of you, and where it goes. Say to yourself silently: I am here, and this is what holds me up.

Two · Breathe

One hand on the belly. Breathe in, and feel it widen. Breathe out, and feel the shoulders fall away from your ears. Three times, slowly.

Three · Shield

Now draw a boundary with the breath. Breathe in, and feel the breath fill the space around you — not a wall, a shape. Breathe out, and feel the shape hold. It is not a barrier. It is a knowing of where you end.

A boundary, not a barrier — that distinction matters and is worth saying out loud. The Air session is the first place protection is taught, and it should not be taught as something that keeps things out.

Closing the practice

Invite people to notice one thing: whether the boundary felt like a wall, a shape, or something they could not find. All three are useful answers, and none of them are wrong.

00:42 Guided meditation 11 min

Eleven minutes, with the Air theme underneath. The instruction is to follow the breath as it actually moves — not to change it, and not to control it.

Settling · 2 min

Let the eyes close, or let the gaze go soft. Let the hands rest. There is nothing to do here, and nothing to get right.
Take three breaths, at the pace that is already there. Notice the breath at the top of the inhale, where it turns. Notice the small pause before the out-breath begins. That pause is the turn of the air.

The breath itself · 4 min

Now follow the breath for a while. Not steering it. Following.

Long pause here — around ninety seconds of silence with the theme playing. The silence is the point of this section, not the talking.

The mind · 3 min

Notice where the mind has gone. It may have gone to tomorrow. It may have gone to something said earlier in the week. Notice it without trying to bring it back. Bringing it back is not the practice.
And notice this: the breath was there the whole time. Wherever the mind went, the breath kept moving. Air does not stop because you are thinking.

Widening · 2 min

Let the attention widen. Out from the breath, into the air around the body — cool on the skin, moving in the room. Out further, into the room itself, and the people in it. You are breathing the same air as everyone else.
Let the eyes open, whenever they are ready.
00:53 Sharing circle 22 min

What surfaced. Unscripted, and the part Linda never prepares. Leave generous space here — in the Water session it was the longest single stretch.

  • Invite in the round, so nobody has to fight for a turn.
  • No fixing, no advice unless asked for.
  • One question to keep the circle moving if it stalls: what changed in the body?
  • Let silence be an answer. Do not rescue it.
01:15 Closing & next steps 10 min

Bring it back to the body — one more breath on the belly, feet on the floor. Then the closing.

Air goes out of the room and into the world, and it will come back in through the window, through the door, through other people. You do not have to hold it. You only have to notice that it is yours.

What to carry into the week

One thing. The grounding practice — feet, breath, boundary — is short enough to do every morning, and it is the one worth taking.
